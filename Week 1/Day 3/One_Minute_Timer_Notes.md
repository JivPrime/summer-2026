# One-Minute Timer — Revision Notes

*(AoE Fig. 1.38, "One Minute of Power", simplified)*

**What it does & how it's built.** This circuit gives a clean output that stays **HIGH for about one minute** after you press a **START** button, then switches **LOW** — a simple one-shot timer. It hangs two branches between a **+5 V** rail and ground. (1) A **timing branch**: START → **R₁ (1 kΩ)** → the node **V_C1** → then **C₁ (10 µF)** and **R₂ (6.2 MΩ)** in parallel down to ground. (2) A permanent **reference divider**: **R₃ (620 kΩ)** over **R₄ (360 kΩ)**, which fixes a threshold **V_ref ≈ 1.8 V** — that value is deliberately **1/e (≈ 37 %) of 5 V**. A **comparator** watches both nodes: its **+ input sits on V_C1**, its **− input on V_ref**, and it outputs **HIGH whenever V_C1 > 1.8 V**. Roles to remember: the timing branch is the *clock*, R₃/R₄ is the *finish line* (threshold), and the comparator is the *referee* that turns a slowly-fading voltage into a clean on/off signal.

**How the voltage and current flow.** Pressing START charges C₁ **fast** to ~5 V through the small R₁ — time constant **τ = R₁C₁ = 10 ms**, so it's full in ~50 ms. Releasing START cuts R₁ off, and C₁ then **discharges slowly** through the large R₂ — **τ = R₂C₁ ≈ 62 s ≈ 1 minute**. Since a discharge falls to 1/e of its start after exactly *one* time constant, **V_C1 crosses the 1.8 V threshold at ~62 s**, so the comparator holds its output HIGH for about a minute and then flips LOW. Key flow facts: **C₁, R₂ and the comparator's + input are the same node**, so they always share the same voltage (V_C1 rises as C₁ charges, falls as it discharges — the + input just *tracks* the cap). The **reference branch draws its own steady ~5.1 µA** (= 5 V / 980 kΩ) from the 5 V source **independently** — an ideal source holds 5 V and feeds each branch separately, so nothing "shares" a fixed current. C₁'s discharge current flows out through **R₂ to ground only**, never into the reference branch (different node). And the comparator input is **high-impedance** (draws ≈ 0 current), so it *reads* V_C1 without draining the capacitor and spoiling the timing.
